The most-taught lessons from one month of live Skylit Stages, written so a trading agent can read them.

- Window: October 26 to November 25, 2026

## The glossary: the words the room uses every day

Each term below carries its plain meaning, why it matters, and a replay link to a moment it was taught (open replay links signed in to Skylit).

### Heat map
Skylit's dealer positioning map. As price moves, dealers are forced to buy or sell to stay hedged.
**Why it matters:** It shows what dealers must do at each level. That is where moves start and stall.
Taught by garma, 2026-10-31 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/96e2a4fe-3bf7-4b33-a8f0-38ccb8f5fb04/replay?t=3135)

### Node
The net exposure at one strike. Bigger size means more weight.
**Why it matters:** Nodes are the levels every play in this doc keys off.
Taught by Larold, 2026-10-31 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/2ea9bf73-d04a-4232-b5d1-4e466c662c21/replay?t=3779)

### Negative node
Volatile fuel. Price tends to speed up and overshoot through these.
**Why it matters:** It pulls price toward it fast. On arrival it can floor the move or snap it back.
Taught by Glitch, 2026-10-28 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/1c461666-a42f-472f-ad84-8fd94c3b42b6/replay?t=4997)

### Node growth
A node getting bigger. It only matters if it keeps growing.
**Why it matters:** Growth means a level is being defended right now, not yesterday.
Taught by Giul, 2026-11-13 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/9a0a5f32-31f9-492d-a51b-996a9aac5084/replay?t=6751)

### Confluence
Two or three of the maps showing the same level.
**Why it matters:** One map can lie. Two agreeing is the room's minimum to trade.
Taught by garma, 2026-10-31 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/96e2a4fe-3bf7-4b33-a8f0-38ccb8f5fb04/replay?t=4315)

### Messy map
Nodes clustered close, equal-size neighbors, price in the middle. The room's cue to stand aside.
**Why it matters:** A clean map is the filter before any trade. No clear king, no trade.
Taught by Boogeyman, 2026-10-30 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/45fa1b40-8751-4841-ac56-37e930e58bf6/replay?t=3072)

### King node
The biggest node on the chart. Only three show per day: SPY, SPX, QQQ.
**Why it matters:** The day's main magnet, and the anchor of the core trade.
Taught by garma, 2026-11-07 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/5812e9ab-df02-40ef-8358-6d2a265e769d/replay?t=964)

### King node deflection
The core Skylit trade: price taps the king node, you take the reversal away from it. Do nothing in the middle.
Taught by Larold, 2026-11-14 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/d8cfa43d-73b3-4a35-9b6c-1c23058473c9/replay?t=545)

### Liquidity sweep
Price takes out obvious stops at a high or low, then reverses.
**Why it matters:** The fake-out before the real move. The month's most-taught entry starts here.
Taught by Forbes, 2026-10-26 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/144a5b29-09da-4b06-9ed5-52fd2b93731c/replay?t=553)

### Golden zone
The fib pullback area where dips often stop.
**Why it matters:** It marks the pullback worth waiting for instead of chasing.
Taught by Boogeyman, 2026-11-02 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/fb69c402-a020-4aa6-a435-29ec50140869/replay?t=1783)

### Break and retest
After a level breaks, wait for price to come back and tap it from the other side.
**Why it matters:** How the room enters a break without chasing it.
Taught by nicog8, 2026-11-11 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/756a6a04-6d8b-43a6-917d-5066f307b5b1/replay?t=6520)

### Opening range
The high and low of the first 30 minutes. The break sets the first lean.
**Why it matters:** The day's first real lean comes from this range breaking.
Taught by nicog8, 2026-10-29 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/ca18602e-fbb5-4a25-bba4-74cddb1df5bd/replay?t=23218)

### Opening reshuffle
Dealers rebuild positions in the first 15 to 30 minutes, so nodes jump around.
**Why it matters:** It is why the room waits out the open before trusting the map.
Taught by garma, 2026-11-04 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/b3cdbc77-37a5-4597-bf25-29c8a4d11811/replay?t=877)

### Pika
The room's name for a yellow, positive node.
**Why it matters:** Same, for the other side of the map.
Taught by Boogeyman, 2026-10-26 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/687ceadc-60d8-4ced-ab6c-95a333183840/replay?t=2659)

### Barney
The room's name for a purple, negative node.
**Why it matters:** You will hear it every session.
Taught by Boogeyman, 2026-10-26 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/687ceadc-60d8-4ced-ab6c-95a333183840/replay?t=2659)

### Rug and reverse rug
A positive node over a negative one warns of a dump. Flipped, it warns of a pump.
**Why it matters:** The map's loudest warning that a drop or a pop is loading.
Taught by garma, 2026-11-10 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/c8ae979e-d3da-4531-85e9-b2a66491b592/replay?t=1378)

### Chasing
Entering late into a move that already happened.
**Why it matters:** The most common way the room sees accounts bleed.
Taught by Glitch, 2026-10-28 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/1c461666-a42f-472f-ad84-8fd94c3b42b6/replay?t=4363)

### Timmy trades
A Skylit order-flow marker for large trades hitting a price.
**Why it matters:** It flags big orders the moment they hit.
Taught by garma, 2026-11-06 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/cf447c83-2114-479b-b15d-71b78c791225/replay?t=7563)

### Chop day
Price whips both ways and goes nowhere.
**Why it matters:** Calling chop early is the difference between a flat day and a bleed.
Taught by garma, 2026-11-09 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/616c365a-2926-47c9-9305-a1b9e10c34b2/replay?t=5185)

### Event day
A session ruled by scheduled news: FOMC, CPI, OPEX, end of month.
**Why it matters:** The maps go unreliable, so the play is size down or sit out.
Taught by nicog8, 2026-10-30 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/459fe6ba-30dd-499b-87c3-272dd8ea8d12/replay?t=3614)

### Trend day
Price picks one direction and keeps it, breaking level after level.
**Why it matters:** Fading costs the most on these days. Spot it early.
Taught by garma, 2026-11-12 -- [replay](https://app.skylit.ai/stages/177f8fa7-3b56-4eaf-b3e4-3fa800f46bbf/replay?t=2139)

### Six more node words, briefly

- **Fresh node.** Newly built, untapped exposure. A node that has already held since the open is weaker.
- **Gatekeeper.** A node in price's path that can slow or stop it before the bigger level behind it.
- **Lit node.** A strike glowing with visibly large exposure. Bright yellow means strongly positive.
- **Stubborn node.** A node that keeps holding or pulling price back, tap after tap.
- **Edge nodes.** Large far-away nodes that signal a big player positioned for a move, like a hedge against bad news.
- **Double shine.** Two expirations stacking exposure on one strike, so the node glows twice as strong.

### Also heard on the Stages

- **Air pocket.** A stretch of strikes with almost no exposure, so price can travel through it fast. A pathway between levels, not a place price stops.
- **Kill zones.** The session windows: Asia 8 PM to midnight Eastern, London 2 AM to 5 AM, New York 9:30 to 4. Each session builds a range the next one trades against.
- **Beach ball.** Price overshooting into a negative node and snapping back, like a beach ball pushed underwater. The overshoot is the setup, not the breakdown.
- **Holy Trinity.** Three positive king nodes agreeing across the three maps at once. The strongest alignment the room trades.
- **Bearish divergence.** Price rising while the king node flips lower. The room's signal of a fake-out pump.
- **Chart first.** Technical analysis before the heat map. Find your own levels, then let the nodes confirm.

## The five skills: what to prioritize

### Reading the Heatseeker maps
Become an expert at reading the Heatseeker maps: where the nodes sit, how they move, and what that says about the next trade. `1,287 Statements` -- led by garma, Giul, nicog8, Larold.
1. The Heatseeker map confirms your chart read. It is not a signal by itself.
2. A setup needs two things: big volume pulling price toward a level, and a strong floor or ceiling there to turn it.
3. Execute on the front expiration. Use the all view for the day's direction.
4. SPY and QQQ maps go stale after the 4 PM close. SPX keeps trading, so its map stays live.
5. Yellow nodes are the deflection spots. Purple nodes pull price in fast, then floor it or snap it back.
6. Messy map, no trade: clustered nodes, equal-size neighbors, price stuck in the middle.
To go deeper: Academy, Heatseeker course. [Chapter 3: Node Hierarchy](https://app.skylit.ai/academy/b0000001-0000-0000-0000-000000000003)

### Setting up your charts
Chart first: find your own levels, then set up Atlas and Trinity so the map confirms your read at a glance. `852 Statements` -- led by garma, Glitch, nicog8, Larold.
1. Chart first: mark support and resistance yourself, then overlay the nodes as confluence.
2. Atlas draws the nodes on the chart as orbs. Orb size is node strength.
3. Limit the nodes you show: the top 5, or 20 to 30 percent of the king node.
4. Trinity is the three-map view. Its triangles show node speed, not size.
5. Timmy trades show only on futures charts. White is sell, blue is buy.
6. Paper trade in Nexus before real money. The voices publish their configs there.
To go deeper: Academy, Futures Clinic. [Charting Key Levels](https://app.skylit.ai/academy/d88ed06c-5707-4418-a32b-644134c0da87)

### Setups and entries
Learn the handful of entry patterns the room actually trades, and take only those. `1,212 Statements` -- led by garma, Giul, Forbes, nicog8.
1. Enter on a reaction at a node, never in the middle of a range.
2. Buy floors, short ceilings. The best price is the defined level.
3. The first tap or first retest is the one to play. Trust fades with each tap after.
4. Break and retest is the standard way to trade a lost floor or a reclaimed ceiling.
5. Stack confirmations: maps agreeing, a sweep, a Timmy, a fib or psych level.
6. No entry in the chop zone between nodes, and never buy straight into a king node.
To go deeper: Academy, Heatseeker course. [Chapter 6: Execution Models, Trading the Deflection](https://app.skylit.ai/academy/b0000001-0000-0000-0000-000000000006)

### Managing yourself
Protect the account from your own habits. The voices call this the real skill. `445 Statements` -- led by garma, Giul, Glitch, nicog8.
1. The biggest skill is standing aside when the map is unclear. Reading the map comes second.
2. No daily dollar goal. A must-make-money mindset forces trades.
3. After a green morning, stop or slow down hard. That is when wins get given back.
4. Two failed tries on one idea is the cap. A third is revenge trading.
5. Set your max losses, max daily loss, and size before the open. Never size by mood.
6. Judge a trade by whether you would take it 10 of 10 times, not by the result.
To go deeper: Academy, Futures Clinic. [Trading Psychology: Six Truths (Plus One)](https://app.skylit.ai/academy/23472cc5-bc1f-4358-99d3-8e590754b068)

### Timing the day
Know which hours help you and which hours hurt you. The clock is its own filter. `298 Statements` -- led by garma, nicog8, Glitch, Larold.
1. Wait out the first 15 to 30 minutes. Dealers rehedge overnight and the maps reshuffle.
2. The opening range reads at 10:00. Flux Field is not fully formed until 10:30.
3. Lunch, about noon to 1:30, is chop. Most voices size down or sit out.
4. The best windows are the first two hours and the last two. 2 to 4 PM beats lunch.
5. The last ten minutes can invert the map as dealers unwind. Size tiny or just watch.
6. Mondays reshuffle hardest. Fridays are called scammy, so de-risk a green week.
To go deeper: Academy, Futures Clinic. [Why Futures: Contracts, Sessions and the Index Link](https://app.skylit.ai/academy/0f1e9488-951f-4f49-837e-b6751406d82a)

## 1. The Playbook: the ten ideas taught most often

### 1. Know the day type before anything else
Chop day, trend day, or event day decides the whole playbook. On chop days the top teaching is to stand aside or fade the range edges. On trend days, never fade. Detectors discussed: map disagreement, node growth, and the size of the VIX move.
`10 voices · 37 Stages` — garma, Glitch, Giul, TheArchitect, Forbes, nicog8, Larold, Boogeyman, thequietcalf

### 2. Liquidity sweep into a node, then reverse
The most-discussed entry of the month. Price wicks through an obvious level (sweeping stops), the move lands on a strong heat-map node, and the trade is the reversal back. A sweep without a node, or a node without a sweep, is a weaker trade.
`9 voices · 44 Stages` — garma, Glitch, Forbes, TheArchitect, nicog8, Larold, Giul, Boogeyman, Calf

### 3. Only trade when the maps agree
SPY, SPX, and QQQ each have their own heat map. Take the trade when at least two of the three show the same level. Size down or skip when they disagree. Taught as the main filter against low-quality signals.
`8 voices · 41 Stages` — garma, nicog8, Glitch, Larold, Giul, Boogeyman, Wicksy, TheArchitect

### 4. The opening-range pullback into the golden zone
Let the first 30 to 60 minutes set the range, wait for the break, then enter on the pullback into the 50 to 78.6 percent retracement of that move instead of chasing the breakout. The most-taught specific entry sequence.
`6 voices · 35 Stages` — garma, Calf, Glitch, nicog8, Boogeyman, TheArchitect

### 5. 0DTE options are a tool with rules of their own
Same-day options only when the day has range; futures on slow or pinned days. Pick strikes near the money, cap time in the trade, and respect that high VIX makes options expensive enough that a correct direction can still lose.
`9 voices · 31 Stages` — garma, thequietcalf, Giul, nicog8, Forbes, Larold, Boogeyman, Glitch

### 6. Node growth is direction information
A node getting bigger is being defended; a node decaying is losing its pull. Do not short while the nodes above price keep growing.
`10 voices · 30 Stages` — garma, Glitch, nicog8, TheArchitect, Forbes, Larold, Boogeyman, thequietcalf, Giul

### 7. The fib golden zone is confluence, never alone
The 0.5 to 0.786 retracement is a place to look for entries only when it lines up with a node or a prior level. Nine voices used it; nobody taught it as a standalone signal.
`9 voices · 30 Stages` — garma, nicog8, Glitch, Boogeyman, Giul, Calf, thequietcalf, Forbes

### 8. The first touch of a node is the best touch
Play the first tap of a fresh level, and get more suspicious as the taps repeat.
`8 voices · 29 Stages` — Anubis, garma, Giul, nicog8, Larold, Forbes, TheArchitect, Boogeyman

### 9. Rugs and reverse rugs
A large negative node flipping or appearing under price (a rug) warns of a drop; one above price (a reverse rug) warns shorts. Taught with a hard rule attached: never fade a reverse rug, and never trade every rug blindly.
`9 voices · 28 Stages` — garma, nicog8, Giul, Larold, Glitch, Boogeyman, reqzzy, Wicksy

### 10. Skip or shrink on event days
FOMC, OPEX, CPI, big earnings, and month-end make the maps unreliable and the tape choppy. The most common version: sit out entirely. The milder version: half size and wider stops.
`10 voices · 27 Stages` — Glitch, nicog8, Giul, garma, Larold, Boogeyman, TheArchitect, thequietcalf, Forbes

## 2. The House Rules

The presenters spent more breath on discipline than on any entry pattern: 662 recorded warnings and reminders across the month. These eight rules were repeated by every one of them.

1. **Never enter without a written plan.** Entry, stop, target, and size are decided before the order goes in. Never after.
2. **Cap the day.** A fixed number of losses or trades ends the session. No exceptions, no winning it back.
3. **Two failed attempts means the idea is wrong.** Stop re-entering the same trade. The market already answered.
4. **No chasing.** If price already made the move, the reward-to-risk is gone. Wait for the retest or skip it.
5. **Respect the clock.** Let the open settle, go easy through midday, and stand down before the close.
6. **Map unclear means no trade.** A no-trade day is a successful day. There is no daily dollar goal.
7. **Keep size constant.** No doubling after losses, no victory-lap sizing after wins. Cool down after both.
8. **Journal every trade.** One record per trade, written at the time it happens. The journal is the edge detector.

## 3. The No List

The 71 hard "do not" rules recorded across the month, grouped by what they protect. Written as gates a bot can enforce mechanically.

### Maps (16)
- Do not fade a reverse rug (a negative node above price).
- Do not trade every rug, or every large print, blindly.
- Do not short while the nodes above price keep growing, and do not short triple floors.
- Do not chase a lit node as a target.
- Skip setups with only single-node confluence.
- Do not take a long when the maps do not support the bounce (many king nodes below the floor).
- When SPY, QQQ, and SPX disagree, expect a trickier day; do not short one map's deflection into another map's floor.
- After a node flips role or rolls under price, do not assume floor or ceiling until confirmed.
- Avoid a positive node with negative stacks on both sides (the sandwich).
- Do not use stale or delayed maps as live levels.
- Ignore map targets far from price when planning.
- Standard deviation bands: do not assume reversion just because price is outside the second band; expect one to three legs; it fails on trend days.
- Do not use flow-anchored VWAP tools as a buy or sell signal on their own, overnight, in the first hour, or on trend days.
- Do not copy someone's levels without knowing why, and do not take a trade just because someone alerted it.
- Do not act on a third-party thesis whose invalidation level was already hit, unless the level was reclaimed.
- Check real distance in points; chart scaling can mislead.

### Entries (12)
- Do not chase: price already ran, the reward-to-risk is gone.
- Do not short without a node to short from, and short weak tickers, not strong ones.
- The breakdown of a lost king node is riskier than the reversal; size light.
- Delta divergence with no node and no liquidity sweep: skip.
- Third tap of a level: skip or size smaller; a fourth tap likely breaks.
- Do not buy calls into a king node hoping it blasts through, and not into a rug.
- Pair a moving-average rejection with a map node before acting on it.
- If you cannot decide long or short and keep flipping back to back, stand aside.
- A limit order placed exactly on a negative node should expect overshoot.
- Do not buy dips on trend-down days, and do not chase shorts after a big drop.
- Do not trade the middle of a range or a chop box, and skip low-volume chop days.
- Do not front-run nodes out of habit; entering early is a deliberate choice with a wider stop.

### Risk (11)
- Never trade without a defined entry, exit, stop, and target.
- Never full-port. Size small on futures, use micro contracts for small accounts, and check the contract symbol.
- If the stop a setup needs is far larger than normal, the setup is not practical in that vehicle.
- Copy-trading across three accounts is one account at three times the size.
- Do not trail a far target too tightly, and expect option trailing stops to get hit by noise.
- Know your prop firm's rules: news windows, daily loss limits, inverse-position rules, and do not hold through FOMC minutes.
- A stop placed just beyond an obvious level may be the liquidity others are buying.
- Do not average down on options.
- Do not try to recover a big loss in one day.
- Do not leave a large trade unattended.
- Do not use inverse or single-setup sizing to chase a dollar goal.

### Time (8)
- Nothing in the last 10 to 15 minutes; the last half hour belongs to the close and the maps reshuffle.
- Do not trade overnight or the Asia session on stale maps; several tools do not work overnight at all.
- Maps are unreliable around earnings, OPEX, month-end, and FOMC; big gaps need a reshuffle before the levels mean anything.
- In the first hour, give the tools time: flow averages need about an hour, option-implied ranges need 30 to 60 minutes, and the maps need a couple of minutes after the open.
- Scheduled news candles print wicks; do not be fully sized into them.
- Do not force trades in a holiday-shortened week, and consider de-risking into a green Friday.
- Do not swing over weekends or overnight in a market like this one.
- Time-of-day rules are general guidelines with exceptions, not laws.

### Options (10)
- High VIX makes options expensive and crush-prone: the right direction can still lose. Plan for it.
- Around FOMC, implied volatility is elevated and options need a bigger move to pay; newer traders should watch.
- When the range is tight or pinned, use futures, not options.
- Do not hold short premium through earnings, sell premium only on names you would own, and mind the volatility term structure.
- For one-day-to-expiry trades, prefer the liquid underlying over the illiquid one.
- Buying at the highs of the day risks a double-digit drawdown on one red candle.
- No iron condors or one-week spreads when VIX is high, and check option liquidity before entering.
- Wide-spread index options can be down double digits instantly on slippage alone.
- Do not call a bottom while the VIX curve is backwardated, and do not draw chart patterns on VIX itself.
- The real risk of a level-based option trade can be several strikes, not one, if the move keeps running.

### Bots (14)
- Do not ask an AI to find an edge from nothing, and do not try to vibe-code a profitable agent.
- Do not rely on an AI's own verdict about alpha or its own backtest numbers.
- Do not leave trade decisions to LLM discretion.
- Do not let the backtest see the future or remember earlier runs.
- Do not trust a backtest alone, a few good days, a 98 percent win rate, one huge trade, or a 20-percent-a-month claim.
- Do not ignore fees, spread, and slippage, and do not compare candles across data sources as if they were the same feed.
- Do not loosen a selective strategy just to get more trades.
- Discount a long-only backtest run in a rising market; do not assume a ten-year-old sample still applies; a six-month sample is too short for positioning studies.
- Treat shared agent output as context, never as a buy signal.
- Treat unvalidated tools and feeds as unproven until you have tested them yourself.
- Do not paste API keys into chat, do not scrape the UI, and expect rate limits and missing data.
- Do not leave an agent linked to an account unmonitored, do not build a swing agent first, and do not build an agent to fix manual unprofitability.
- Do not build on unreleased features.
- Do not stack many indicators and keep the one that happened to work.

## 4. Skybots: what the room teaches about agentic trading

Agent building was its own thread through the month: 48 of the 77 Stages touched it, with 381 on-record statements from 15 voices, led by garma and Glitch. The teaching was consistent: the bot's job is not to be smarter than you. Its job is to follow the rules perfectly.

1. **Never ask an AI to find an edge from nothing.** The bot executes frozen, human-defined rules. It does not invent strategies.
2. **No AI discretion mid-trade.** Entries and exits follow the written rules; the model never improvises with money on the line.
3. **Never grade your bot on its own word.** An AI's claim that a backtest passed is not a backtest. Check the numbers yourself, from the raw fills.
4. **The bot's best job is enforcement.** It follows your plan perfectly, caps your day, and tells you when you are being undisciplined.
5. **Compare every signal against dumb luck.** A member study of 787,368 entries found many touch "hit rates" sat within a few points of a random-walk control. A win rate means nothing without that comparison.
6. **Grade on the minute path, not the daily bar.** Daily-bar grading quietly flatters results; in that same community testing it invalidated about a third of claimed wins.
7. **Costs flip results.** One shared strategy went from +69R to -13.8R with a quarter percent of slippage. Signal accuracy and after-cost profit are different questions. Exits, fees, and fills earn the money.
8. **Count every test you run.** The best result out of fifty tries is luck until it survives a forward test. Keep a ledger of every attempt, including the failures.

## 5. How to use this with your agent

1. Drop this file into your agent's context as reference material, not as a strategy to trade.
2. Treat every Playbook idea as a hypothesis. Backtest it on your own data, with costs, before it touches even a paper account.
3. Where presenters disagreed on a number (a retracement level, a stop distance, a time window), test the range as a sweep. Do not pick one person's number and call it settled.
4. Wire the House Rules and the No List in as hard gates. They are the part of this guide a bot can enforce today, with no edge required.
5. Keep your own ledger of every test. Report the count beside any result you share.
